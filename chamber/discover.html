Discover Huancavelica

A highland region in Peru known for Andean culture, stunning landscapes, and living traditions.

Local Culture

Huancavelica blends Andean heritage, traditional textiles, music, and community celebrations. Visitors can experience local markets and regional dishes while supporting local producers.

Historic City Center

Huancavelica Cathedral, also known as the Main Church of San Antonio, is one of the oldest and most important buildings in the historic center of the city. Its construction began in the early 17th century during the Spanish colonial period. The cathedral was built by Catholic religious orders with the purpose of establishing religious authority and serving as a spiritual center for the growing mining city of Huancavelica. Over time, it has been restored after earthquakes and today stands as a symbol of faith, history, and colonial architecture in the region.

Andean Lagoons

Choclococha is a high-altitude Andean lagoon located in the Huancavelica region at more than 4,600 meters above sea level. It is one of the largest lagoons in the area and plays an important role in the local ecosystem and water systems. Surrounded by vast grasslands and mountains, the lagoon is traditionally considered an ancestral and sacred place by nearby communities. Today, it is valued for its natural beauty, biodiversity, and potential for eco-tourism and landscape photography.

Mountain Viewpoints

The Sachapite Stone Forest is a unique natural attraction located near the city of Huancavelica. It is formed by large rock formations that rise up to 20 meters high, shaped over thousands of years by volcanic activity and natural erosion caused by wind and rain. Many of the stones resemble animals, human figures, or ancient monuments, making the site both visually striking and culturally fascinating. The area is ideal for hiking, exploration, and learning about the geological history of the Andes.

Traditional Festivals

The Scissors Dance is a traditional Andean ritual dance widely practiced in Huancavelica. It features dancers, known as danzaq, who perform complex and acrobatic movements while rhythmically striking two metal blades that resemble scissors. The dance is accompanied by live harp and violin music and represents a symbolic competition of skill, endurance, and spiritual strength. With origins in pre-Hispanic Andean culture, the Scissors Dance was recognized by UNESCO in 2010 as Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, highlighting its deep cultural and historical significance.
